Privacy

What the maker of Listen receives about you, which is as close to nothing as software can get. Last updated 14 August 2026.

The product

Listen runs entirely on your devices. Jacaranda Labs operates no server for it, requires no account, and collects no telemetry, analytics, or crash reports. Your recordings, transcripts, notes and voiceprints are yours, on hardware you control, and we cannot see them.

If you enable iCloud sync, data moves between your own devices through your own Apple account, sealed with a key we never hold. If you point the Ask feature at a hosted AI provider, the transcripts you ask about go to that provider under your agreement with them, not ours. Both are off until you turn them on.

What we incidentally touch

Some infrastructure sees ordinary connection metadata, such as your IP address, because of how the internet works:

  • GitHub serves the update check (every two days), the downloads, and this website (GitHub Pages). GitHub's own privacy policy governs its logs; we run no analytics on any of it.
  • Hugging Face serves the one-time speech model download.
  • Apple handles App Store and TestFlight distribution of the iPhone app, under Apple's terms.

None of these connections carries your recordings, transcripts, or anything derived from them.

Your responsibilities as the recorder

You are the controller of what you record. Recording other people is regulated nearly everywhere: consent rules differ by country and by US state, and workplaces have their own. Listen gives you the tools (deletion that propagates everywhere, voiceprint forgetting, an activity log), and the obligation to record lawfully is yours.

Data rights

Every right a data subject might exercise against a Listen library is exercised on the device that holds it, by its owner: the library is open WAV and JSON files, so access and portability are the file system, and deletion is deletion, propagated to every synced device and to iCloud. There is no copy at Jacaranda Labs to ask about, correct, or erase.
