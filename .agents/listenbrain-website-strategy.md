# listenbrain.app Website Strategy

*Research and working direction — 9 September 2026*

## Recommendation in One Sentence

Position Listen as **the private, conversation-first second brain your AI can
actually use**: it learns the people, projects and decisions in the
conversations the user chooses to keep, then returns useful context with a path
back to what was actually said.

The site should not hide recording. Recording is the comprehensible input and
the source of trust. It should stop treating recording and transcription as the
ultimate product.

## The Message Spine

1. **Pain:** The context that makes you good at your work lives in conversations
   and then disappears.
2. **Problem:** Notes are incomplete, transcripts are piles of text, and every
   AI starts without the personal context that would make it useful.
3. **Mechanism:** Listen turns chosen conversations into an evolving memory of
   people, projects, decisions and history.
4. **Payoff:** You remember better, prepare faster, follow through, and give your
   AI the context only you have.
5. **Reason to believe:** Every important detail can be checked against the
   original conversation, corrected by the user and kept on the user's devices.

This is the website's version of pain → problem → solution. It keeps the useful
part of the supplied sales-funnel diagram without importing an infoproduct
style built around “10× bonuses,” order bumps and aggressive purchase pressure.

## What the Reference Sites Teach Us

### Granola: product-led clarity and proof

Granola's homepage currently opens with a familiar category — “The AI notepad
for back-to-back meetings” — followed by the compact promise “Notes, actions and
memory. Without a meeting bot.” It immediately shows the product, adds a short
trust/compatibility strip, then puts recognizable customer logos before the
longer explanation.

The rest of the page is structured around a customer's day rather than a
feature inventory:

1. Before the meeting: arrive prepared.
2. During the meeting: give it your attention.
3. After the meeting: remove the admin.
4. Perfect meeting memory: ask across the accumulated history.
5. Customer proof.
6. Compatibility, privacy, mobile and calendar details.
7. AI/MCP extension: use meeting context in other tools.
8. Repeated low-friction download CTA.

The transferable lesson is not Granola's colors or typography. It is that every
abstract claim is paired with a recognizable situation and a product artifact.
The page lets visitors see themselves using it before asking them to understand
the architecture.

Granola's separate Chat page is even closer to Listen's intended direction. It
leads with “AI chat that actually understands your work,” then demonstrates
questions, outputs and documents inspired by conversations. This validates
moving from “capture a meeting” to “use the accumulated context,” while keeping
the meeting workflow as the acquisition wedge.

Sources:

- https://www.granola.ai/
- https://www.granola.ai/chat

### nanoBrain: a sharp enemy and a legible mechanism

nanoBrain leads with “The second brain that thinks like you,” then names a
specific deeper problem: decisions, voice and relationships are scattered
across a person's head, notes and AI sessions, so every new agent must be
brought up to speed. It then explains a memorable three-part mechanism —
capture, distill, query — before comparing itself with alternatives and
answering technical objections.

What Listen should borrow:

- Name the enemy: **every AI starts from zero**.
- Explain the system in three ordinary verbs.
- Show examples of what the memory contains and what the user can ask.
- Use a comparison to make the new category concrete.
- Answer ownership and privacy objections plainly.

What Listen should not borrow:

- “Knowledge corpus,” command lists and infrastructure terminology in the main
  story.
- A long architecture-first page aimed at developers.
- Claims broader than the conversations Listen actually captures.

Source: https://nanobrain.app/

## The Framework to Use

No single copywriting framework is sufficient. Use them in sequence:

### 1. April Dunford: decide what the product is compared with

Use *Obviously Awesome* before writing copy:

1. What would the customer use if Listen did not exist?
2. What can Listen do that those alternatives cannot?
3. What customer value follows from those differences?
4. Who cares about that value most?
5. What market category makes the value obvious?

Current working answer:

- Alternative: mental memory, partial notes, a meeting summarizer and repeatedly
  briefing an AI.
- Unique capability: source-backed, correctable memory built from calls and room
  conversations and available to the user's chosen AI.
- Value: continuity across people/projects and AI output that starts with the
  right personal context.
- Best-fit customer: an Apple-using independent operator whose effectiveness
  depends on recurring conversations.
- Category: conversation-first personal second brain.

Source: https://www.aprildunford.com/books

### 2. Jobs to Be Done: find the language and struggling moment

The page should be built around the moment someone reaches for a solution:

- Ten minutes before a recurring client call, they cannot remember what was
  left unresolved.
- An old decision resurfaces and nobody remembers why it was made.
- They open Claude or Codex and spend the first prompt explaining the project,
  people and prior conversations again.
- Someone mentions a personal detail they feel they ought to remember.

These moments produce better copy than demographic labels or abstractions. They
also give us an interview script: ask what happened last time, what they tried,
what was frustrating and what changed their behavior.

Source: https://jobstobedone.org/

### 3. StoryBrand: keep the customer as the hero

Use the useful StoryBrand sequence without making the page theatrical:

- Character: a capable person carrying too much context.
- Problem: important conversations disappear and AI lacks the context.
- Guide: Listen quietly captures and organizes what the user chooses.
- Plan: capture → remember → use.
- Call to action: start building your Listen memory.
- Failure: repeated explanations, lost decisions and relationships reconstructed
  from fragments.
- Success: arrive prepared, follow through and use AI that understands the
  relevant history.

Listen is not the hero and the monkey is not the hero. The user becomes the
person who remembers.

Source: https://storybrand.com/

### 4. Conversion anatomy: show, prove, ask once

Use the practical landing-page layer described by Unbounce: a clear value
proposition, a product hero/demo, benefits, social proof and one primary
conversion goal. For Listen, that means no competing “Source,” “Read the docs,”
“Join Discord” and “Download” buttons in the hero. Those links can remain in the
navigation or footer, while the page repeats one primary CTA.

Sources:

- https://unbounce.com/landing-page-articles/the-anatomy-of-a-landing-page/
- https://unbounce.com/conversion-rate-optimization/the-state-of-saas-landing-pages/

## Recommended Homepage Structure

### 1. Minimal navigation

Logo, How it works, Privacy, Download/Pricing, and one primary CTA. Keep product
source, technical documentation and GitHub accessible but out of the main
conversion path.

### 2. Hero: outcome, category and immediate proof

**Eyebrow:** A private second brain for your conversations

**Recommended headline:**

> The personal second brain your AI can actually use.

**Subhead:**

> Listen turns the conversations that shape your work and life into a living
> memory of people, projects and decisions — private, correctable, and linked to
> what was actually said.

**Primary CTA:** Download Listen for Mac

**Secondary action:** Watch how it remembers

**Under-button reassurance:** No account · Apple silicon · Your audio stays on
your devices

**Hero visual:** A silent 20–30 second product loop, not a generic illustration:

1. A conversation finishes.
2. The person's brief gains a sourced detail and a project link.
3. The user asks “What should I remember before speaking to Alex?”
4. Listen answers with two clickable sources.
5. Claude or Codex uses the selected context to draft the next piece of work.

This is the entire positioning demonstrated rather than explained.

### 3. Immediate credibility strip

Until approved customer/company logos exist, use honest product proof:

- Calls and in-person conversations
- Mac and iPhone
- Answers linked to the source
- Works with the AI tools you already use

Replace this with approved customer proof when available. Do not invent logos,
review counts or company adoption.

### 4. The struggling moment

**Heading:** The best context never makes it into your notes.

Suggested copy:

> The promise made at the end of a call. Why the team rejected the first plan.
> The detail a client mentioned three months ago. The exact words behind a
> decision. These are the things that make your next move better — and the first
> things ordinary notes lose.

Then show three concrete costs:

- You reconstruct old decisions from fragments.
- You walk into recurring conversations underprepared.
- You keep teaching powerful AI tools the same context from scratch.

### 5. The mechanism: three verbs

**Heading:** Listen. Remember. Use.

1. **Listen:** Record a call on your Mac or a conversation on your iPhone.
2. **Remember:** Listen learns who spoke and builds a living history of the
   people, projects and decisions you choose.
3. **Use:** Ask your memory directly or give relevant context to Claude Code,
   Codex and compatible AI tools.

Every step needs a real product crop or short motion example.

### 6. What the second brain remembers

Frame capabilities as outcomes:

- **People:** Preferences, responsibilities, relationships and what changed.
- **Projects:** Decisions, open questions, history and the reasons behind them.
- **Promises:** What you or somebody else said they would do.
- **Your thinking:** The notes you wrote that no transcript could infer.
- **The source:** Who said it, when, and the conversation it came from.

### 7. High-value scenarios

Use three recognizable scenes, each with an exact question and a visible
answer:

**Before the next conversation**

> “Catch me up before I speak to Maria again.”

**When an old decision returns**

> “Why did we decide against annual pricing?”

**When AI needs your context**

> “Draft the project brief using what customers actually said in these calls.”

This section is more persuasive than a grid of generic features because it
connects stored memory to a moment where the visitor already feels the pain.

### 8. The AI multiplier

**Heading:** Stop starting every AI conversation from zero.

Explain in plain language first:

> Your AI can be brilliant and still give you a generic answer. Listen gives it
> the relevant conversations, decisions and people context — without making you
> paste a folder of transcripts into every prompt.

Then introduce MCP and supported agents as the technical reason this works.
Never lead the section with the acronym.

### 9. Trust: a memory this personal should belong to you

This is where privacy becomes a differentiator:

- Recording and transcription can stay on the device.
- There is no required Listen account or meeting bot.
- Owner sync is encrypted before the data reaches iCloud.
- Hosted AI is optional and its boundary is disclosed before use.
- Generated memory can be checked, corrected, excluded or deleted.

Show the actual privacy/settings interface or a small data-flow diagram. Avoid
generic shields and padlocks.

### 10. Comparison: what kind of memory do you want?

Use a restrained comparison with categories rather than a hostile competitor
scorecard:

| | Meeting summary | Notes/second-brain app | Platform AI memory | Listen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starts from real conversations | Yes | Sometimes/manual | Only what you tell it | Yes |
| Remembers people/projects over time | Limited | Manual | Within one vendor | Yes |
| Links claims to the source | Sometimes | Sometimes | Rarely | Yes |
| User can correct the memory | Limited | Yes | Limited | Yes |
| Available to chosen AI tools | Limited | Varies | Usually no | Yes |
| Local/private path | Varies | Varies | Usually cloud | Yes |

Every public comparison must be re-verified before launch and use precise,
qualified wording.

### 11. Customer proof

The current site correctly keeps placeholder testimonials hidden. The new page
should launch proof only when permission exists. The best proof is specific:

- What kind of conversation the person records.
- What they stopped doing.
- What became possible after several weeks of accumulated memory.
- A recognizable name/role or privacy-appropriate attribution.

One end-to-end case story will do more work than ten generic praise cards.

### 12. How to start

Keep setup to three visible steps:

1. Download Listen on your Mac.
2. Record one conversation and name the people.
3. Ask what was said; let the memory grow from there.

If the iPhone app or paid version has a separate acquisition path, route after
the primary Mac conversion instead of presenting two equal products in the
hero.

### 13. FAQ and final CTA

Answer the anxieties that block conversion:

- Is it recording all the time?
- Does anyone else receive my audio?
- How is this different from a meeting notetaker?
- How does it know what is true?
- What happens when something changes?
- Can I delete or correct what it learned?
- Which Macs, iPhones and AI tools work with it?
- What does it cost?

Finish by repeating the single CTA with the success state:

> Build the memory you wish every AI already had.

## Visual Evidence and SmoothCapture Production Plan

The product is mature enough that the site should be built around real proof,
not interface mockups or abstract second-brain illustrations. Each major claim
should be followed by the smallest real interaction that proves it.

### The primary visual story

Create one 20–30 second silent hero loop that tells the whole positioning story:

1. A conversation finishes in Listen.
2. A person or project brief gains a new, sourced detail.
3. The user asks, “What should I remember before speaking to Alex?”
4. The answer appears with references and one source is opened.
5. A selected piece of context is used in Claude Code or Codex to produce the
   next piece of work.

This should feel like one continuous cause-and-effect sequence, not a feature
montage. The recording screen establishes where the memory came from; the
brief, Ask answer and source prove what makes Listen more than a recorder; the
AI handoff proves the final payoff.

The hero loop must still make sense when muted and when only its poster image is
visible. Keep a “Watch the full demo” action for visitors who want narration.

### Supporting product assets

Produce four or five focused 6–10 second loops. Each should prove one claim and
live beside the copy making that claim:

- **It recognizes people:** name a speaker once, then show Listen suggesting the
  same person in a later conversation.
- **The memory grows:** a person brief gains a sourced detail or change after a
  new conversation.
- **Answers are checkable:** ask across several conversations, then open the
  exact source behind one part of the answer.
- **Calls and rooms become one memory:** capture on iPhone, then show the result
  in the same library or person/project history on Mac.
- **The user stays in control:** correct, hide, exclude or delete a generated
  detail and show the visible result.

Add four to six high-resolution stills for visitors who scan, reduced-motion
users, social sharing and search previews:

- a person brief showing Details and History;
- an Ask answer with numbered references;
- a project view showing decisions or changes over time;
- the iPhone capture/library experience;
- the real privacy or data-control interface;
- the AI-tool handoff, if it remains legible in a still.

A narrated 60–90 second demo can sit lower on the page. It should follow one
person and one project through a believable workflow rather than touring every
screen.

### Capture direction

Use a dedicated demo library containing realistic but invented people,
companies and conversations. It should have enough history to make the product
feel genuinely useful, while guaranteeing that no customer, employee or private
conversation appears in public media.

Keep one visual preset across every asset:

- the same window size, crop, corner radius, background and padding;
- a clean desktop with notifications, personal accounts and unrelated menu-bar
  items removed;
- natural pointer movement and real clicks so automatic zooms have meaningful
  targets;
- key clicks and answers at normal speed, with waiting and transcription time
  shortened between them;
- a brief still moment before and after each important interaction so the edit
  remains readable;
- Mac and iPhone footage framed consistently instead of presented as unrelated
  product tours.

Capture at Retina or 4K source quality, then export for the actual display size.
Keep a master SmoothCapture project for each workflow so text, timing, zooms and
output formats can be refreshed after product releases without recreating the
story from scratch.

### SmoothCapture workflow

SmoothCapture is a strong fit because it can record a Mac window, a real iPhone
or the iOS Simulator; create zooms from actual click events; add backgrounds,
frames, cursor treatment and annotations; and export video, GIF and stills. Its
command-line workflow can also make repeated captures and exports consistent.

Recommended production sequence:

1. Build the fictional demo library and write a click-by-click shot list.
2. Save one Listen website preset in SmoothCapture for framing and styling.
3. Record one clean master take for each workflow, including extra pause at the
   beginning and end.
4. Edit zooms and timing once in the SmoothCapture project.
5. Export a web video, a poster image and any alternate crop from the same
   master.
6. Re-run the capture after material UI changes instead of leaving stale product
   footage on the site.

Before automated capture begins, the SmoothCapture command-line control needs to
be enabled once and macOS Screen Recording permission needs to be granted by the
user once. After that, saved presets and scripted capture/export can make the
asset pipeline repeatable.

Source:

- https://www.smoothcapture.app/
- https://www.smoothcapture.app/features/cli-agent-automation

### Web delivery rules

Use modern compressed video for most motion. A GIF is useful only when the loop
is extremely short and its final file is genuinely smaller; conventional GIFs
are often too heavy and lose color quality. Every motion asset needs a poster
image.

Implementation requirements:

- `autoplay`, `muted`, `playsinline` and `loop` for silent micro-demos;
- pause or replace decorative loops when `prefers-reduced-motion` is active;
- controls and captions for any narrated or longer video;
- descriptive alternative text or adjacent copy that communicates the same
  outcome without motion;
- load below-the-fold video only when it approaches the viewport;
- preserve aspect ratio and reserve dimensions to avoid layout shift;
- measure the hero asset on a real mobile connection and compress until it does
  not delay the headline or primary CTA.

Working asset manifest:

| Asset | Purpose | Preferred outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hero-memory-loop` | Full capture → memory → Ask → source → AI story | MP4/WebM plus WebP poster |
| `recognize-person` | Cross-conversation speaker recognition | MP4/WebM plus WebP poster |
| `person-memory-source` | Brief update and supporting evidence | MP4/WebM plus WebP poster |
| `ask-open-citation` | Verifiable answer | MP4/WebM plus WebP poster |
| `iphone-to-memory` | Room conversation joining the same memory | MP4/WebM plus WebP poster |
| `correct-your-memory` | User control and trust | MP4/WebM plus WebP poster |
| `listen-full-demo` | Narrated end-to-end story | MP4/WebM, captions and poster |

The final paths should follow the new website repository once its location is
chosen. Do not mix production assets into the app repository by default.

## Positioning Angles to Test

### A. Conversation-first second brain — recommended category

**Promise:** Your important conversations become a useful memory that grows
with you.

**Best for:** Broad product story and the `listenbrain.app` domain.

**Risk:** “Second brain” is broad. Always qualify it through conversations and
show exactly what the product remembers.

### B. Stop starting AI from zero — recommended acquisition message

**Promise:** Give Claude, Codex and other AI tools the personal context that
makes their answers useful.

**Best for:** AI-native founders, consultants and technical power users.

**Risk:** It can make Listen sound like infrastructure. Lead with the human pain
of repeating context and show a normal question, not a protocol diagram.

### C. Remember the person, not just the meeting

**Promise:** Carry the relationship forward: preferences, history, promises and
what changed.

**Best for:** Consultants, coaches, recruiters and advisers.

**Risk:** Can sound like a CRM or surveillance. Emphasize explicit capture,
source visibility and user control.

### D. A memory you can trust

**Promise:** AI-speed recall without surrendering the record behind it.

**Best for:** Privacy-sensitive and high-consequence conversations.

**Risk:** Trust is a differentiator, not enough of an initial desire by itself.
Pair it with preparation, follow-through or better AI work.

### E. Be the person who remembers

**Promise:** Arrive prepared, notice patterns and make people feel remembered.

**Best for:** Emotional brand storytelling and testimonials.

**Risk:** It is compelling but less explicit about the product. Use below the
hero or in a campaign once category comprehension is established.

### F. One memory across Mac and iPhone

**Promise:** Calls at the desk and conversations in the room become one
continuing memory of the same people, projects and decisions.

**Best for:** Making Listen's full capture surface tangible and differentiating
it from products built only around scheduled desktop meetings.

**Risk:** “Available on all your devices” can sound like ordinary compatibility.
Show the continuity of one real workflow—Mac call → iPhone room conversation →
one sourced answer—rather than a row of platform logos.

## Recommended Message Combination

Do not choose only one angle. Give each a job:

- **Category:** A private, conversation-first second brain.
- **Hero/payoff:** The personal second brain your AI can actually use.
- **Pain:** Every AI starts from zero, and the context is trapped in
  conversations.
- **Emotional outcome:** Be the person who remembers.
- **Differentiation:** Every important detail links back to what was actually
  said.
- **Trust:** Your conversations stay under your control.
- **Continuity:** Mac hears the call, iPhone hears the room, and both become one
  private memory.

## Plain-Language Copy Rules

| Internal/product language | Customer-facing language |
| --- | --- |
| context engine | a memory you can ask |
| provenance | where this came from |
| temporal facts | what changed, and when |
| semantic retrieval | finds the idea even when you use different words |
| entity graph | how people and projects connect |
| incremental extraction | keeps selected memories up to date |
| MCP server | use your Listen memory in the AI tools you already use |
| personal CRM | remember the person, without maintaining a CRM |
| LLM provider | the AI you choose |

Every section should pass three tests:

1. Would a customer say this sentence aloud?
2. Does it name a recognizable moment or result?
3. Can the product visibly prove it?

## What to Test Before Polishing the Design

1. Five-second comprehension: after seeing the hero, can someone explain what
   Listen does without saying only “meeting recorder”?
2. Message preference: second brain vs AI context vs relationship memory.
3. Example resonance: which question makes someone say “I need that”?
4. CTA intent: download now vs watch demo vs early access.
5. Trust threshold: which privacy or accuracy objection appears before someone
   will record a real conversation?

The first design should support easy copy and section-order experiments. Do not
turn the initial concept into a brittle one-off visual composition.

## Open Decisions

- Final primary audience and the first use-case story.
- Whether “personal second brain” appears in the H1 or only in the eyebrow and
  metadata.
- Whether Speak/dictation is part of the new product story.
- Packaging, pricing and primary CTA.
- Which customer quotes and company names have explicit permission.
- Whether the commercial site replaces or sits beside the current open-source
  GitHub Pages site.
- Whether Mac + iPhone continuity leads the homepage or supports the broader
  conversation-first story.
