# Asking for a quote

The section is built and sitting in `index.html` behind a `hidden` attribute.
Two real quotes is enough to turn it on. Delete the placeholder cards, put the
real ones in their place, and remove `hidden` from `<section id="voices">`.

## Ask the people who already said it

The strongest quotes are already written. Somebody has told you, unprompted,
that they love recording WhatsApp calls from their Mac. That sentence is the
testimonial. Go back to that message and ask whether you can use it.

**Do not ask friends to invent one.** A quote with no recognisable name and no
specific detail reads as filler, and a reader who spots one stops believing the
rest of the page. An empty space costs less than a hollow quote.

## Always ask for the original message

Ask for what they actually typed, not for a summary of it, and not for your own
memory of it. Daniel's first quote here went through four rewrites, each one
tidier and less persuasive, before anybody looked at what he had really written:
*"I've tested many similar apps out there, and Listen is finally the one that
stuck."* Nobody drafting on his behalf would have produced that sentence, and
it does more work than everything written to replace it.

The real message is messier and better. Every time.

## What makes one usable

- **One specific thing they do.** "I record client calls on WhatsApp and the
  transcript is there before I have closed the window" is worth more than
  anything with the word *seamless* in it.
- **The trade they stopped making.** The best line on Pocket's whole site is a
  customer's: "I can focus on clients, Pocket writes my notes." It names the
  thing somebody gave up before, which is more persuasive than any feature.
  If a user says something in that shape, take it.
- **Their words, kept.** Fix a typo. Do not smooth the grammar, and do not make
  three people sound like one copywriter.
- **Attribution a stranger can weigh.** A name, what they do, and a city.
  For a therapist or a lawyer, role and city alone is better, and offering that
  first makes it far easier to say yes.
- **Short.** Two sentences. Cut the rest.

## A message that works

> Hey. You mentioned you have been using Listen for your WhatsApp calls, which
> made my week. Would you mind if I put that on the site?
>
> No pressure at all, and no need to write anything new: I would just quote what
> you already said. If you would rather not have your name on it, "therapist,
> Rotterdam" works just as well, and if you would rather not at all, that is
> completely fine and changes nothing.

Three things make it easy to say yes: they write nothing, they can stay
anonymous, and refusing is explicitly free.

## Before it goes up

- Show them the exact wording and attribution you plan to use, in place.
- Ask again if you later reword it.
- Keep a copy of them agreeing. A screenshot of the reply is plenty.
- If they stop using it and ask you to take it down, take it down.

## What not to do

- No invented quotes, no composites, no quotes from people who have not used it.
- No logos of companies that have not agreed to appear.
- No star ratings or review counts unless they come from somewhere real that a
  reader can go and check.
- Nothing that identifies a client, a patient or a matter somebody discussed on
  a recorded call. That is the one mistake this product cannot survive.
