Free and open source · Apple silicon · macOS 14 or later

This looks like an Intel Mac, and Listen will not run on it.

Listen transcribes on your own machine, and that needs the Neural Engine Apple introduced with the M1 in late 2020. There is no Intel version, and there is no version that would work.

Open the Apple menu and choose About This Mac. If it says Chip rather than Processor, this guess is wrong and you can download Listen as normal.

What it does

Seven things, one at a time.

Pick one, or let it run.

Record

Both sides of the call, and no bot in it.

Your microphone on one track, everything your Mac plays on the other. Nothing joins the call, so it works on WhatsApp and FaceTime as well as it does on Zoom, Meet and Teams. Press record when the call starts, and decide whether to keep it when you stop.

  • Recording starts the moment you press it, so you don't lose the first minute
  • Walk away without deciding and the audio deletes itself after 24 hours
  • Asks to record audio, not your screen

Transcribe

Written up before you've made coffee.

Transcribing happens on your Mac's own chip, about 240 times faster than real time on an M4 Max. Play a meeting back and the transcript follows along, sentence by sentence, so you can find the bit you half remember without listening to the rest.

  • Fix one sentence without touching the paragraph around it
  • Reads 25 languages, and works out which one it's hearing
  • Teach it the names and jargon it keeps getting wrong

Speakers

Name someone once. Listen knows them next time.

Listen works out who spoke and puts names on the turns. Tell it who someone is once, and it recognises them in the next meeting they turn up in. On a call it never has to guess at your own voice.

  • Works for a room too, with the laptop on the table
  • Fix a whole speaker, one paragraph, or one sentence
  • Voiceprints are biometric data, and yours never leave your Mac

Ask

Ask anything. Check every answer.

“What did we decide about pricing?” “Catch me up.” Ask one meeting or all of them. Every claim comes with a numbered reference, and clicking it takes you to the recording it came from. If a model answers without reading anything, Listen says so.

  • Runs on a model on your own Mac, if you'd rather it did
  • Or through Claude Code, Codex, and twelve services set up for you
  • Nothing is ever asked in the background

Notes

Your notes stay yours.

Write your own notes while the meeting is still running, which is when they're worth the most: “we should upsell them” is the kind of thing no transcript will ever hold. Ask an agent for a summary and it writes a separate one. It can read yours. It cannot change them.

  • No New Note button. Open a recording, click Notes, start typing
  • One note can cover four meetings, and outlive any of them
  • Tag a meeting in your own words, and ask for that tag later

Dictate

Talk anywhere on the Mac, and it types.

Press fn + left shift wherever you're typing, say what you want written, and press it again. The words land where the cursor is, in an email, in Slack, in a search bar, and on the clipboard as well.

  • The same model, and the same vocabulary, your meetings use
  • On macOS 26, an optional pass that drops the ums
  • Needs Accessibility. Recording meetings never does, so it never asks

Your devices

Start on one Mac, pick up on the other.

Turn on sync and your transcripts, notes, people and tags follow you between Macs, through your own private iCloud rather than anybody's server. Listen seals all of it on the device first, with a key Apple never holds. There's nothing to pair and no code to scan.

  • The audio travels too. An hour of meeting is about 61 MB
  • No device deletes its copy until another one has it
  • Anything a sync removes is kept for fourteen days

An iPhone app, for the conversations that happen in a room rather than on a call, is on the way.

Agents

Use your meetings anywhere.

No more pasting transcripts into a chat window. Connect Claude, or anything else that speaks MCP, and it can read your library directly.

claude mcp add listen -- /usr/local/bin/listen mcp

It can write notes and tags. That's all. It cannot rename a speaker, correct a transcript, retitle a recording or delete one. A wrong note sits harmlessly beside the recording that disproves it. A wrong transcript edit is a fact that's simply gone, so those edits stay with you.

Listen doesn't need to be running for any of it, and there's a full command line with the same boundary.

Privacy

There is no Listen server.

Nothing to hold your meetings, nothing to breach, nothing to hand over. They are saved on your own computer, and the recording, the transcript and the names on it are all made there. No account, and nothing reports back.

  • It works with the Wi-Fi off. None of it waits on a connection, so losing the network does not cost you the meeting.
  • Your recordings are ordinary files. A folder you own, that you can move, back up or delete. There is nothing to export, because nothing was ever taken.
  • Keep everything, for as long as you like. Storing your own meetings on your own disk costs nothing, so nothing is ever deleted to make you pay.

A couple of things can send anything out, and each is off until you turn it on: keeping your library in step across your own devices, and putting a question to a hosted model. Listen seals anything that travels before it leaves the device, with a key we never hold, so there is nothing readable at the far end. Point the questions at a model running on your own Mac and even those stay put.

None of which has to be taken on trust. Listen is open source, so the code is the claim. What runs where sets out every connection it can make and how to check each one yourself, and there are answers ready for a privacy or HIPAA review.

Somewhere with rules about this? Listen can be locked down centrally through the same device profiles an IT team already uses: sync off, questions answered only by a model on the machine, no dictation history, backups pointed wherever policy says. Deploying under HIPAA or GDPR has the detail.
